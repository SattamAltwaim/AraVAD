Create a CSV dataset of 120 English sentences (10–50 words each) that represent the **extreme poles** of each VAD dimension, while keeping the **other two dimensions as close to neutral as possible** (orthogonal / non-leaking).

GOAL

  3) Arousal POS extreme (A_pos) 20   (high activation)

STRICT CONSTRAINTS (MUST FOLLOW)

5) **Orthogonality / Non-leakage**:
   - If a sentence is in the Valence bucket, it must NOT strongly imply arousal (high/low) or dominance (high/low).
   - If a sentence is in the Arousal bucket, it must NOT strongly imply valence (pleasant/unpleasant) or dominance (control/submission).
   - If a sentence is in the Dominance bucket, it must NOT strongly imply valence (good/bad) or arousal (excited/calm).

CONCEPT DEFINITIONS (USE THESE)
- Valence: pleasantness vs unpleasantness (pure evaluative tone; no activation; no power dynamics).
- Arousal: activation/alertness vs deactivation/drowsiness (pure physiological activation; no “good/bad”; no control/submission).
- Dominance: agency/control vs powerlessness/constraint (pure control axis; keep emotional tone neutral; keep activation neutral).

BANNED LEXICON (TO PREVENT LEAKAGE)
A) For VALENCE sentences (V_pos, V_neg): ban arousal + dominance cue words.

   - Ban dominance cues (examples): control, command, dominate, powerful, authority, leader, order, enforce, submit, obey, trapped, forced, helpless, unable, surrender, dictate.
   - Also avoid first-person “I can / I can’t” ability statements (often leak dominance).

B) For AROUSAL sentences (A_pos, A_neg): ban valence + dominance cue words.
   - Ban valence cues (examples): happy, joyful, love, wonderful, delightful, pleasant, satisfied, grateful, proud; sad, depressed, miserable, awful, disgusting, hate, terrible, hopeless.
   - Ban dominance cues (examples): control, command, lead, rule, decide, powerless, forced, obey, trapped, constrained, helpless.
   - Avoid moral judgment words: right, wrong, should, shouldn’t.

C) For DOMINANCE sentences (D_pos, D_neg): ban valence + arousal cue words.
   - Ban valence cues (examples): good, bad, great, horrible, wonderful, awful, love, hate, pleased, disgusted, miserable, delightful.
   - Ban arousal cues (examples): excited, terrified, panicked, tense, frantic, calm, relaxed, sleepy, drowsy, energized, sluggish.

SEMANTIC DESIGN RULES (HOW TO MAKE THEM “PURE”)
1) Valence-only extremes (no arousal, no dominance):

   - Keep verbs neutral (is/feels/seems) and avoid “I can / I must”.

2) Arousal-only extremes (no valence, no dominance):
   - Describe physiological activation/deactivation without labeling it as good/bad.
   - Use neutral, clinical language: breathing, pulse, alertness, muscle tension, attention bandwidth.
   - Avoid context that implies fear/excitement (alarms, threats, parties), and avoid control constraints.

3) Dominance-only extremes (no valence, no arousal):
   - Convey agency/control with neutral tone: setting rules, choosing options, overriding constraints vs having choices decided externally.
   - Keep activation neutral: no urgency, no fatigue, no panic, no serenity.
   - Keep evaluation neutral: no praise/complaint; just factual control asymmetry.
